Frequency and Wavelength: Formula and Examples

Frequency is the number of cycles per second, while wavelength is the distance between corresponding points of a wave.

Formula

λ = v / f, where λ is wavelength, v is wave velocity and f is frequency.

Example: 100 MHz

For an electromagnetic wave travelling about 300,000,000 m/s at 100 MHz, wavelength is about 3 metres.

Example: 2.4 GHz

At 2.4 GHz, free-space wavelength is approximately 12.5 cm.

Practical Note

Use the appropriate wave velocity. Electromagnetic waves travel close to the speed of light in free space, while velocity can be lower in materials.

FAQs

What happens when frequency increases? For constant velocity, wavelength decreases.

Can this formula be used for sound? Yes; use the speed of sound for the relevant medium.
