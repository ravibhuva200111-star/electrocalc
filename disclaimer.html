Electrical Safety — Read This If You're Doing Real Wiring Work

Calculators like Wire Gauge / Voltage Drop, PCB Trace Width, and Resistor Power Rating touch on electrical safety. A few important limitations to understand:

  • Voltage drop is not the same as ampacity. A wire gauge that shows an acceptable voltage drop percentage may still be undersized for safe current-carrying capacity depending on insulation type, ambient temperature, conduit fill, bundling with other cables, and installation method. Always check ampacity tables (e.g. NEC Table 310.16 or your local equivalent) separately.
  • Local electrical codes vary by country, state, and municipality. Formulas here are general-purpose and do not account for your specific jurisdiction's code requirements (NEC, IEC, BS 7671, or others).
  • Real-world installation conditions matter. Temperature derating, altitude, humidity, cable grouping, and conduit material can all affect safe current ratings beyond what a simple calculator captures.
  • Always size overcurrent protection (breakers/fuses) appropriately for the actual wire ampacity, not just the calculated load.

For any installation involving mains voltage, building wiring, or anything where an error could cause fire, shock, or equipment damage, consult a licensed electrician or professional engineer and verify against your local electrical code and manufacturer datasheets.
