Home solar and battery sizing, explained step by step.

Work out how many panels, how big a battery and which inverter your home needs, using your own electricity bill and four simple formulas.

The four sizing steps

Sizing a home system follows the same order every time: how much you use, how big the solar array must be, how big the battery must be, and how big the inverter must be. Our worked example uses a home that uses 300 units a month.

Step 1: find your daily energy use

Your electricity bill shows monthly units, and one unit is one kilowatt-hour. Divide by 30 to get daily use. See our power guide for how to estimate individual appliances.

300 units / 30 days = 10 kWh per day

Step 2: size the solar array

Solar output depends on how many peak sun hours your location gets, which is the equivalent number of hours of full-strength sunshine per day. Many places in India see roughly 4 to 6, but check a local figure. Real systems lose some energy to heat, dust, wiring and the inverter, so use an efficiency factor of about 0.75.

kWp = Daily kWh / (sun hours × 0.75)
Panels = kWp × 1000 / panel watts

10 kWh a day, 5 peak sun hours:

kWp = 10 / (5 × 0.75) ≈ 2.67 kWp

Using 550 W panels:

Panels = 2,670 / 550 ≈ 4.9, so round up to 5 or 6 panels

With 6 panels (3.3 kWp), expected daily output is:

3.3 × 5 × 0.75 ≈ 12.4 kWh per day

Step 3: size the battery

Decide how much energy you want from the battery. This is usually your evening and night use, or the loads you want to keep during a power cut. Batteries should not be fully drained, and the inverter wastes a little energy, so divide by both factors.

Battery kWh = Need / (DoD × inverter efficiency)

5 kWh of evening use, 90% usable depth of discharge, 92% inverter efficiency:

Battery = 5 / (0.90 × 0.92) ≈ 6.0 kWh

So look for a battery of about 6–7 kWh. Learn more about these ratings in our BESS guide.

How long will the battery run my home in a power cut?

A 7 kWh battery feeding a 500 W load (lights, fans, Wi-Fi, fridge):

Usable energy = 7 × 0.90 × 0.92 ≈ 5.8 kWh

Runtime = 5.8 / 0.5 ≈ 11.6 hours

Our Battery Life calculator does the same arithmetic for any load.

Step 4: size the inverter

The inverter must cope with everything running at once, plus the brief surge that motors draw when they start. List the loads you expect to run together and add a margin of about 25% to 50%.

A 1.5 kW air conditioner, a 0.2 kW fridge and 0.3 kW of fans and lights:

Running load = 1.5 + 0.2 + 0.3 = 2.0 kW

Inverter ≈ 2.0 × 1.5 = 3 kW

The electrical side: current and cables

Low-voltage battery systems carry large currents. On a 48 V battery bank, a 3 kW load draws:

I = P / V = 3,000 / 48 ≈ 62.5 A

Thick short cables and correctly rated fuses are essential on the DC side. Use our Wire Gauge calculator to check voltage drop. Panels and batteries produce DC and the home uses AC, which our AC vs. DC guide explains.

What could it save?

If solar covers 300 units a month, at an example tariff of ₹7 per unit:

300 × 7 = ₹2,100 per month

Tariffs, net-metering rules and subsidies differ by state and utility, so check your own before deciding.

Common mistakes

  • Sizing by roof space. Size by your energy use first, then see what fits.
  • Using kWp as real output. Apply losses of roughly 25%.
  • Ignoring shade and dust. Even partial shade can cut output sharply.
  • Mixing kW and kWh. Inverters are rated in kW and batteries in kWh.
  • Forgetting motor surges. Air conditioners and pumps need a bigger inverter than their running power.

Practice problems

Try each one before you open the answer.

1. A home uses 450 units a month, with 5 sun hours and 0.75 efficiency. What kWp is needed?

Daily use = 450 / 30 = 15 kWh. kWp = 15 / (5 × 0.75) = 4 kWp.

2. How many 540 W panels make 4 kWp?

4,000 / 540 ≈ 7.4, so 8 panels.

3. You need 8 kWh from the battery at 90% DoD and 92% inverter efficiency. What battery size?

8 / (0.90 × 0.92) ≈ 9.7 kWh.

Frequently asked questions

How many solar panels do I need for my home?

Divide your daily energy use in kWh by the peak sun hours and an efficiency factor of about 0.75 to get the array size in kWp, then divide by the wattage of each panel.

What is the difference between kW and kWp?

kWp is the rated peak output of the solar panels under standard test conditions. kW is a general power unit. Real output is usually lower than kWp because of heat, dust, wiring and inverter losses.

How big a battery do I need?

Size it for the energy you want to use after sunset or during an outage, then divide by the usable depth of discharge and the inverter efficiency.

Can solar panels run my home without a battery?

A grid-tied system works without a battery but shuts down during a power cut for safety. To keep power during outages you need a hybrid inverter with a battery.
