AC vs DC, explained with everyday examples.

Why your wall socket and your phone battery use different kinds of electricity, what RMS and frequency mean, and how chargers and inverters convert between them.

The core difference

DC: direct currentFlows one way at a steady voltage, like a river. Batteries, solar panels, USB ports and most electronics use it.
AC: alternating currentReverses direction many times a second, like waves going back and forth. Wall sockets and the power grid use it.

Frequency and period

Frequency is how many full cycles AC completes each second, measured in hertz (Hz). India and much of the world use 50 Hz, while the US uses 60 Hz. The time for one cycle is the period.

T = 1 / f
f = 1 / T

Mains at 50 Hz:

T = 1 / 50 = 0.02 s = 20 ms per cycle

The direction reverses 100 times every second.

What "230 V AC" really means: RMS

AC voltage is constantly changing, so we quote one effective number, the RMS (root mean square) value. It is the DC voltage that would deliver the same heating power to a resistor. For a sine wave:

Vpeak = VRMS × 1.414
VRMS = Vpeak / 1.414

A 230 V mains supply:

Vpeak = 230 × 1.414 ≈ 325 V

That is why insulation and capacitors in mains equipment must be rated well above 230 V.

Why the grid uses AC

Power loss in a cable is I² × R, so lower current means far less wasted heat. Since P = V × I, raising the voltage lowers the current for the same power. Transformers raise and lower AC voltage easily and efficiently, which made AC the winner for long-distance transmission.

Delivering 1 MW (1,000,000 W):

At 230 V: I = 1,000,000 / 230 ≈ 4,348 A

At 11 kV: I = 1,000,000 / 11,000 ≈ 91 A

The current is about 48 times lower, so the cable loss is about 2,300 times lower (48²) for the same cable.

Converting between AC and DC

  • Rectifier (AC to DC): diodes block the reverse half of the wave. A capacitor and regulator then smooth it. Every phone charger and laptop adapter contains one.
  • Inverter (DC to AC): electronics switch a DC source rapidly to make an AC output. Home inverters and solar systems use them.
  • Transformer (AC to AC): steps AC voltage up or down. It does not work on steady DC.

Practical example: sizing an inverter battery

A 1,000 W load run from a 12 V battery through an inverter that is 90% efficient:

DC input power = 1000 / 0.9 ≈ 1,111 W

Battery current = 1111 / 12 ≈ 92.6 A

That heavy current is why low-voltage inverter systems need thick cables. Our Wire Gauge calculator helps size them, and the Battery Life calculator estimates runtime.

Ohm's Law and AC

Ohm's Law works directly for AC on purely resistive loads such as heaters and incandescent bulbs, using RMS values. With capacitors and inductors, resistance becomes impedance (Z) and current can lag or lead the voltage. See the Ohm's Law guide for the basics.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up RMS and peak. Multimeters read RMS. Peak values are about 1.414 times higher.
  • Plugging DC gear into AC, or the reverse. Check the label for input type and voltage first.
  • Expecting transformers to work on DC. They need the changing magnetic field that only AC provides.
  • Touching "low" AC. Mains voltage is dangerous. Always switch off and isolate before working on wiring.

Practice problems

Try each one before you open the answer.

1. What is the peak voltage of a 120 V RMS supply?

120 × 1.414 ≈ 170 V.

2. What is the period of a 60 Hz supply?

T = 1 / 60 ≈ 0.0167 s, or 16.7 ms.

3. A 500 W load runs from a 12 V battery via a 100% efficient inverter. What current does the battery supply?

I = 500 / 12 ≈ 41.7 A. A real inverter would draw more because of losses.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main difference between AC and DC?

DC current flows in one direction at a steady voltage, like from a battery. AC current reverses direction many times a second, so its voltage swings up and down.

Why do power grids use AC?

AC voltage can be stepped up or down easily with transformers. High voltage means lower current, which cuts the heat lost in long transmission lines.

What does 230 V AC actually mean?

It is the RMS value, the steady DC voltage that would heat a resistor equally. The actual voltage peaks at about 325 V.

How do phone chargers turn AC into DC?

A charger steps the voltage down, rectifies it to DC with diodes, then smooths and regulates it to a steady output such as 5 V.
