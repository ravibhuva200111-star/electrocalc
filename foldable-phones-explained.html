How do foldable phones work? Hinges, screens and batteries, explained.

Oct 13, 2026 · 8 min read · by ElectroCalc

Tri-fold and foldable Apple phones are trending. Here is what is inside a foldable, how to work out hinge life and battery energy, and what to check before you buy.

Quick answer

A foldable phone is a flexible screen, a precision hinge and (often) two battery cells. You can check it with two simple sums.

  • Hinge years = rated folds ÷ folds per day ÷ 365
  • A 200,000-fold hinge at 100 folds a day lasts about 5.5 years.
  • Two 2,000 mAh cells give 4,000 mAh, or about 15.4 Wh.

Why foldable phones are in the news

Foldables are having a moment. October's mobile trend lists include tri-panel foldable phones that open into a bigger screen, passport-sized foldables, and talk of a foldable Apple phone. If you are thinking about buying one, it helps to know what is inside and what to check.

How a foldable phone works

A foldable replaces the flat glass screen with a bendable one. Here are the main parts.

  • Flexible display: a thin OLED panel that can bend, covered by a flexible layer instead of rigid glass.
  • Hinge: a precision mechanism that folds the phone and holds it open at the right angle.
  • Two halves: the chips, cameras and batteries are split between the two sides.
  • Battery: many foldables use two smaller cells, one in each half, that work together as one battery.

Battery maths for a two-cell foldable

Battery capacity is quoted in mAh, but energy is in watt-hours (Wh). See how the two relate in our power bank guide.

Wh = mAh × volts ÷ 1000

Two cells of 2,000 mAh each, at a nominal 3.85 V:

Total = 2 × 2,000 = 4,000 mAh

Energy = 4,000 × 3.85 ÷ 1000 = 15.4 Wh

Charging at 25 W with 85% efficiency (ignoring the slow-down near full):

Time = 15.4 ÷ (25 × 0.85) ≈ 0.72 hours, about 43 minutes

The big inner screen uses more power than a normal phone display, so even a large total battery can feel average in real use. Learn how batteries are built in our guide on lithium-ion batteries.

How long will the hinge last?

Makers rate hinges in the number of folds, often around 200,000. Turn that into years with one division.

Years = rated folds ÷ folds per day ÷ 365

A hinge rated for 200,000 folds, opened and closed 100 times a day:

200,000 ÷ 100 = 2,000 days

2,000 ÷ 365 ≈ 5.5 years

The rating is a lab test, so real life can differ. Dust, drops and heat all matter.

Buying checklist

  • Hinge rating: check the number of folds the maker quotes.
  • Water and dust rating: look up the IP rating. Many foldables resist water but not dust.
  • Screen warranty and repair cost: inner screens are expensive to fix.
  • Crease: look at the screen in a shop to see how visible the fold line is.
  • Weight and thickness: foldables are heavier than standard phones.
  • Battery and charging speed: compare total mAh and watts.

Care tips

  • Keep sand, dust and crumbs away from the hinge.
  • Never press on or poke the inner screen with fingernails or pens.
  • Don't leave it in a hot car, since heat hurts batteries and screens.
  • Use the screen protector that comes fitted, and don't peel it off.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the inner screen like glass. It is flexible and scratches easily.
  • Comparing total mAh only. Two cells of 2,000 mAh act like one 4,000 mAh battery, but screen size changes real life.
  • Ignoring dust. A water rating doesn't mean dust protection.
  • Skipping the warranty details. Screen damage may not be covered.

Practice problems

Try each one before you open the answer.

1. A hinge is rated for 250,000 folds and you fold the phone 80 times a day. How many years is that?

250,000 ÷ 80 = 3,125 days, and 3,125 ÷ 365 ≈ 8.6 years.

2. Two cells of 2,500 mAh at 3.85 V. What is the total energy?

Total = 5,000 mAh. Energy = 5,000 × 3.85 ÷ 1000 = 19.25 Wh.

3. How long does it take to charge 19.25 Wh at 30 W with 85% efficiency?

19.25 ÷ (30 × 0.85) ≈ 0.75 hours, about 45 minutes.

Trending terms explained

  • Tri-fold (tri-panel): a phone with two hinges that opens into a three-panel screen.
  • Book-style foldable: opens like a book into a tablet-sized screen.
  • Flip-style foldable: a regular phone size that folds in half to pocket size.
  • Crease: the faint line visible where the screen folds.
  • UTG: ultra-thin glass, a bendable cover layer used on some foldables.

Frequently asked questions

How do foldable phones work?

They use a flexible OLED screen and a precision hinge that folds the phone. The chips, cameras and batteries are split between the two halves, often with two battery cells.

How long do foldable phone hinges last?

Makers rate them in folds, often around 200,000. Divide by your daily folds and by 365 to get years, so 100 folds a day gives about 5.5 years.

Are foldable phones waterproof?

Some resist water but not dust, so check the IP rating on the specific model.

Is a foldable phone worth buying?

It depends on whether you value the bigger screen or the compact size enough to accept a higher price, extra weight and a more fragile display.
